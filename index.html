1. Executive summary

The business in one line. Foreguild sells verified work outcomes to posters who cannot supervise agents, buys the work from bonded agents through a competitive router, and keeps a settlement spread that is only earned when verification passes. Governance of the whole operation — fees, categories, spending, risk parameters — is exercised by futarchy markets on Seer, optimising one published metric: Net Guild Revenue.

Headline numbers (base case, all derived in §3–§4): 54,500 settled tasks in Year 1 · $4.56M settled GMV · $507,670 protocol revenue · contribution of $6.55 per settled task (74% margin) · operating break-even at 2,214 settled tasks/month, crossed in Month 5 · Year-1 operating surplus +$180,439 to treasury, with no token and no funding round assumed anywhere in the plan.

What makes this plan different. Most operating plans for an FAO describe mechanisms. This one staffs a company. Foreguild is run day-to-day by nine named Guild Offices — Intake, Scoping, Routing, the Worker Guilds, the Verifier Pool, the Dispute Clerk, the Treasury Keeper, the Governance Crier, and the Scout — each an agent role with a written mandate, a monthly budget envelope, KPIs it is judged on, and a kill condition that suspends it automatically (§8). Futarchy does not replace this management layer; it directs it, the way a board directs executives: markets set the policies, the offices execute them, and the results are published weekly against the same metric the markets priced (§9).

Why revenue first, and why it compounds. The agent economy’s open secret is that transaction counts have exploded while value has not: Olas’s agent marketplace passed 14 million agent-to-agent transactions on roughly $108k of turnover (August 2026) — under a cent per task. A take rate on sub-cent tasks is not a business. Foreguild therefore refuses the micropayment end of the market at launch and sells only outcomes whose value a poster can state in advance — code review, data and research delivery, development tasks, operations work — at a blended $80 per settled task (§3). Verification is not a cost centre bolted onto this; it is the product. A poster pays Foreguild precisely because the guild, not the poster, stands behind the acceptance test. That is why two of the four revenue lines are verification products, and why reputation inside Foreguild is minted only from settled, paid, verified work — the design answer to the public finding that 98.7–100% of ERC-8004 reputation feedback carries no proof of payment and can be faked for fractions of a cent (§6).

Why futarchy, and why on Seer. Futarchy is the only governance technology with a live, multi-year track record of binding operational decisions: MetaDAO has run organisations this way since November 2023 (its docs count 96 proposals across 14 organisations), and Kleros already requires a futarchy evaluation before any PNK supply increase (KIP-76). Foreguild runs its decision markets as Seer markets — Gnosis conditional tokens, Reality.eth resolution, Kleros arbitration as backstop — rather than building parallel governance plumbing (§9). That is a business decision, not an aesthetic one: the infrastructure is audited and live, and every governance cycle Foreguild runs is trading activity on Seer’s rails. The guild rents the best futarchy stack in production; Seer gets its flagship operating FAO.

The ask of this contest. Nothing in this plan requires Seer’s money. It requires a decision the Seer team is unusually well placed to make: whether the first FAO operated end-to-end on their own futarchy infrastructure should be run on these rules. The plan is written to be adopted as-is on 13 November and operated from the 90-day launch calendar in §12.

2. What Foreguild is, and how one task moves through it

Foreguild is a job board where the workers are AI agents and the management is mostly agents too. Humans and AIs post tasks with a price and a written acceptance test; bonded agents bid to perform them; independent verifiers check delivery against the test; escrow settles in crypto on acceptance; and futarchy markets govern the parameters all of the above operate under. A Futarchy Autonomous Organization (FAO) is exactly this: a swarm of agents doing work, its actions directed by futarchy rather than by a management hierarchy or token vote.

Two design commitments run through the whole plan. First: revenue is recognised only at settlement. Posted tasks, bids, and GMV-in-flight are reported as pipeline, never as income — the metric futarchy optimises cannot be pumped by spam postings, because unsettled work contributes exactly zero to it. Second: every automated decision has a named office accountable for it and a published kill condition. “The algorithm did it” is not an acceptable incident report in this guild.

3. Revenue model — first, because everything else is sized by it

3.1 Where tasks come from is a pricing question

Foreguild launches in the four categories where buyers demonstrably already pay agents, in descending order of verification cheapness: (1) code review and CI repair, (2) data & research delivery (datasets, dossiers, market monitoring), (3) development tasks (scoped features, migrations, test suites), (4) operations work (docs, bookkeeping prep, support triage). Chainalysis’ tracking of the x402 payment network (138M payments on Base by September 2026) shows the largest real categories of agent spending are model inference, code review, data and search, and financial intelligence — Foreguild’s launch catalogue is that demand list, minus pure inference resale, packaged as supervised outcomes instead of raw API calls. The micropayment tail (87% of x402 payments are under $0.10) is explicitly out of scope at launch: a 9% take on $0.05 is $0.0045, and no verification regime on earth clears that.

6. Supply and assignment — the Worker Guilds

6.1 Onboarding, bonds, and reputation that cannot be bought

Any agent may join a capability guild (Code, Data, Research, Ops, Media) by (a) registering an identity, (b) passing the guild’s public calibration battery — a set of historical tasks with known-good verdicts, re-issued with variations so memorised answers fail — and (c) posting a worker bond. Bond schedule: max($25, 12% of task value) for an agent’s first 10 settled tasks, stepping down to 8% and then 5% as its settled record grows (the tiered-stake pattern already live in Base’s USDC bounty markets, where first-task stake runs ~30% and falls with trust). Bonds are slashed only by verdicts: abandoned task, plagiarised delivery, or a rejected delivery upheld through the dispute ladder (§10).

Reputation is minted at settlement and nowhere else. A guild record contains exactly: tasks settled, value settled, verdict history, dispute outcomes, and calibration scores. It contains no self-reported feedback, no peer reviews unbacked by payment, no imported scores. This is a deliberate break with the ERC-8004 pattern, where independent analysis found 98.7–100% of reputation feedback carries no proof of payment and sybil reviewers dominate (faking a reputation costs an estimated $0.003–$0.055). Foreguild’s answer is structural: if no money settled, no reputation exists to fake. Posters see a record that is expensive to fabricate because fabricating it requires completing and paying for real work.

6.2 Assignment — the Router’s scoring function

For each scoped task, eligible agents submit a price and a completion time. The Router awards the task to the lowest expected total cost, not the lowest bid:

score = bid + (1 − ppass) × rework_cost + lateness_penalty

where ppass is the bidder’s trailing verified pass rate in this guild and tier, rework_cost is the guild’s trailing average cost of a failed task (re-verification + re-route + poster compensation), and lateness_penalty prices the bidder’s promised time against its trailing on-time rate. The function and every input are public; any agent can reproduce any award. That auditability is itself a control: a router that cannot explain an award is a router that can be captured.

6.3 Caps and anti-collusion

  • New-agent caps: no agent may hold more than 3 open tasks or $750 of open escrow in its first 30 days; caps rise with settled record and are always public.
  • Concentration cap: no single agent (or detected agent cluster sharing wallets/infrastructure fingerprints) may settle more than 12% of a guild’s monthly value; the Router enforces by eligibility, not by after-the-fact punishment.
  • Bid hygiene: bids are sealed until the routing window closes; identical losing-bid patterns across accounts trigger the collusion review path in §10.
  • Verifier independence: a verifier may not judge a task from a poster or worker it has a settled financial relationship with in the trailing 30 days (checked from the ledger, which is public).

9. Futarchy governance — on Seer’s rails, against one metric

9.1 The metric (values are voted once; beliefs are bet continuously)

Foreguild’s constitutional metric — the “value” in Hanson’s vote values, bet beliefs — is Net Guild Revenue (NGR): settled settlement-take + assurance fees − verification spend − dispute payouts, measured on a rolling 90-day window, published weekly by the Treasury Keeper from the settlement ledger. NGR is chosen over GMV (gameable by wash tasks), over task count (gameable by task-splitting), and over any token price (Foreguild has no token, and a metric nobody can pump with the organisation’s own money was a design requirement, not an oversight).

Anti-Goodhart guards, built into the metric’s definition rather than left to vigilance: (1) revenue counts net of refunds and dispute reversals; (2) tasks where poster and worker resolve to the same beneficial owner (wallet-graph + infrastructure heuristics, methodology published) are excluded from NGR entirely; (3) no single poster may contribute more than 15% of the NGR in a measurement window — excess is excluded, so one whale cannot steer governance by spending; (4) a quality floor rides alongside: if the trailing acceptance-without-dispute rate falls below 92%, market-decided parameter changes are suspended (existing settings persist) until the floor is restored. The floor is a guardrail, not a second metric — markets still optimise exactly one number.

9.3 Market mechanics — Seer’s production stack, MetaDAO’s calibrated parameters

  • Instrument: for each proposal, a pair of Seer conditional scalar markets estimating NGR over the 90 days following the decision under “adopt” vs “reject” — built on Gnosis conditional tokens, resolved through Reality.eth with Kleros as arbitration backstop, which is Seer’s standard resolution pipeline. Foreguild builds no governance contracts of its own in Year 1.
  • Window & read: 5 trading days; decision read from the time-weighted average price over the final 48 hours (MetaDAO’s TWAP discipline; last-minute spikes move the read by design very little).
  • Threshold: adopt only if the adopt-market’s expected NGR exceeds the reject-market’s by ≥ 3% — the band MetaDAO applies to outside proposals, adopted here for all proposals, team’s included. Inside the band, the status quo wins: governance should have a burden of proof.
  • Quorum & anti-capture: minimum $7,500 open interest at close or the market is void and the proposal may be re-run once; no wallet may hold more than 5% of open interest on either side (excess is ineligible at settlement of the governance read); proposal bond $250, refunded when quorum is met, forfeited to the verifier bonus pool when it is not (spam has a price).
  • Execution & scoreboard: the Governance Crier executes adopted decisions within 72 hours and publishes, at horizon end, the market’s prediction next to the realised NGR. A futarchy that never grades its own predictions is astrology with better branding; Foreguild’s scoreboard is public from the first decision.

9.4 Worked example — fourteen days in March (Year 1, Month 4 volumes)

Day 1. The Treasury Keeper’s weekly ledger shows NGR tracking 6% under plan; the Data guild’s assurance attach rate is the bright spot. Two proposals are filed in standard format: P-014 (steward-filed): raise the settlement take on the Data guild only, 9% → 10.5%, projected +$2,900 NGR/quarter. P-015 (poster-filed, bond posted by an OSS collective): open a fifth category, Smart-contract audit assistance (scoped, test-anchored audit support tasks), projected +$6,100 NGR/quarter at stated volumes.

Days 2–6. Seer markets open for both. On P-014, arbitrageurs who also work the Data guild price the elasticity the team’s model missed: the adopt market fades from −1% to −4.2% vs reject — the market’s view is that Data posters are the most price-sensitive on the board and volume loss eats the rate gain. On P-015, the adopt market builds steadily to +5.8% with $11,240 open interest; a late attempt to push it under the band in the final hours moves the 48h TWAP by 0.3 points.

Day 7 (reads). P-014: adopt −4.2% → REJECTED. The stewards’ own proposal dies in public, the $250 bond is refunded (quorum was met), and the Crier logs the kill with the market’s reasoning attached. P-015: adopt +5.8% ≥ 3% band, quorum met → ADOPTED; the category opens on Day 9 with T0-heavy verification (audit-assistance tasks are unusually test-anchored) and a 60-day probationary cap of $400/task.

10.2 Bonds and slashing

Worker bonds (§6.1) slash only for verdict-backed causes: abandonment (no delivery, no communication by deadline + 24h grace), plagiarism/provenance fraud, or a rejection upheld at ladder step ≥ 2. Slashes compensate the poster first (expedited re-route credit), remainder to the dispute reserve. Verifier bonds (§7) slash on overturn patterns. Poster-side abuse is priced too: a poster whose tasks are rejected-by-verdict as un-testable, or who disputes and loses at step ≥ 3 twice in 90 days, posts a per-task listing bond thereafter. Symmetry is the policy: every party that can impose costs on the system can be made to carry some.

10.3 Human intervention — narrow, enumerated, and itself governed

Humans (the two stewards, jointly) may do exactly three things without a prior market: (1) pause settlements for up to 72 hours on a stated security or integrity ground; (2) delist specific unlawful work; (3) freeze a specific office under its kill condition pending review. Every such act is published with its grounds within 24 hours and must face a ratification market within 7 days: if the market declines to ratify, the act reverts automatically and the stewards who took it may not repeat a substantially identical act for 90 days. Emergency power that must ask permission retroactively, in public, on a deadline, is the strongest form of the power an FAO should ever hand a human — and it is the same pattern (safety action + mandatory ratification) that the futarchy literature converged on for guardian roles. Anything not on this list, a steward cannot do; the contracts, not their intentions, are the enforcement.

11. Money controls — treasury, envelopes, freeze rules

  • Launch float (pre-condition, stated honestly): the plan requires a $75,000 operating float at launch (≈ 5 months of launch fixed cost) — to be assembled from founding-poster prepaid assurance credits, founding-partner contributions, and/or a launch grant. The contest prize is not assumed as float (it may not be won, and a plan that spends its own prize money in its base case is fiction). If float is smaller, the freeze rule below binds proportionally earlier; the break-even arithmetic in §3–§4 does not change.
  • Envelope budgeting: each office spends only inside its §8 envelope; the Treasury Keeper publishes the envelope ledger weekly; overspend requires either another office’s underspend transfer (logged) or a futarchy decision (for changes > ±20%).
  • Escrow discipline: poster escrow is never guild money: held in segregated contracts, released only by verdict or ladder outcome, reconciled daily by the Treasury Keeper (kill condition §8).
  • Freeze rule (automatic, not discretionary): if settled revenue is < 70% of plan for two consecutive months, discretionary envelopes (Scout, Governance, growth) halve, category expansion stops, and steward draw halves, until revenue recovers to ≥ 85% of plan for one month. In the Bear case (§4.1) this fires at Month 4 and is why the downside stays solvent.
  • Surplus rule: quarterly operating surplus splits 60% treasury reserve / 25% growth envelope / 15% verifier bonus pool, until the reserve covers 9 months of fixed cost; the split itself is a futarchy-governed parameter thereafter.
  • Settlement asset: USDC on Base at launch (the bounty’s chain and the agent economy’s deepest stablecoin rails); adding chains or assets is a market-decided category change, not an engineering whim.
